Project 1 · NLP & Machine Learning

Job Postings: Skill Extraction & Salary Modelling

What do employers ask for in data roles, and can a posting's text help estimate pay? I cleaned raw Adzuna job-posting data for Data Scientist, Data Analyst and BI Analyst roles, built a rule-based skill extractor, and trained a salary model evaluated with leakage-aware cross-validation.

30skills in the custom taxonomy
$19.2Kcross-validated MAE vs $25.1K baseline
0.27R² vs −0.11 for the baseline
0content groups split across CV folds

What I did

  • Data quality audit. Found that every description was exactly 500 characters and cut off mid-text, meaning the source API returns snippets, not full postings. I documented this up front and carried it through every conclusion.
  • Careful deduplication. Postings with identical text but different IDs and locations were tagged with a content group ID rather than deleted, then used to prevent train/validation leakage.
  • Skill extraction with regex. Built patterns for tools, cloud platforms, ML libraries and analytics concepts. Reviewed every match in context and caught a false positive where the language "R" matched inside a requisition number, then fixed the pattern.
  • Salary model. TF-IDF text features, location and search-term encodings in a Ridge regression pipeline, tuned with nested grouped cross-validation. Compared against a mean-predictor baseline and excluded salary columns to avoid target leakage.
  • Deployment checks. Saved the pipeline with joblib and tested it against missing columns, unseen categories, null values, wrong types and empty input.

Findings

  • Dashboards (23.3% of postings) and data visualisation (13.3%) were the most-mentioned concepts in the available text.
  • Skills were detected in 14 of 30 postings (46.7%). Detected skill count had a weak positive link with estimated salary (Spearman 0.29).
  • The tuned model cut error by about $5.9K (23%) against the baseline.
Honest limits. Thirty postings with truncated text and mostly source-predicted salaries makes this a proof of concept, not a market estimate. Zero detections mean "not in the snippet", not "not required". The next step is collecting full descriptions and a much larger sample.

Project 2 · Time Series & Forecasting

Retail Demand Forecasting

Overstocking ties up cash and understocking loses sales. Using two years of retail inventory data, I explored demand patterns and built forecasting models at both the whole-business and single product-store level.

73,100daily records analysed
5 × 20stores × products
3model families compared
30 daysforecast horizon

What I did

  • Exploratory analysis. Aggregated daily sales and examined trend, monthly and day-of-week seasonality, and category contribution.
  • Feature engineering. Extracted date features and encoded store, product, category, region, weather and seasonality fields.
  • Random Forest regressor. Predicted units sold from the full feature set (MAE 7.23, R² 0.99 on a random 80/20 split).
  • SARIMA(1,1,1)(1,1,1,7). Fitted a weekly-seasonal model for one product at one store and projected 30 days ahead.
  • XGBoost with time-aware tuning. Built lag and calendar features and searched 24 parameter combinations using TimeSeriesSplit, which keeps training data strictly before validation data.

Recommendations drawn from the work

  • Pilot forecasting in one high-volume store, then scale.
  • Retrain monthly or quarterly as demand shifts.
  • Surface forecasts in a live Power BI dashboard for buyers and store managers.
What I would tighten next. The 0.99 R² comes from a random split on features that include same-day fields such as the existing demand forecast and units ordered, so it is an optimistic upper bound rather than a true forward-looking score. The stronger version holds out the final weeks chronologically, drops same-day inputs, scores SARIMA and XGBoost on that same holdout, and benchmarks against a seasonal-naive forecast.

Project 3 · Procurement Analytics

Procurement Spend Analysis

Where is the money going, who is it going to, and where can it be saved? I analysed a year of purchasing transactions and turned the results into supplier segments, a risk view, a savings estimate and an executive dashboard.

$1.24Mtotal spend analysed
500purchase orders, 2024
26.5%of spend with top supplier
~$50Kestimated savings opportunity (4%)

What I did

  • Validation and KPIs. Checked types, nulls and duplicates, then built an executive KPI summary.
  • Pareto (ABC) analysis of suppliers and categories. Electronics alone is 56.2% of spend and, with Software, makes up over 83%.
  • Supplier risk matrix plotting spend against order activity to separate strategic, operational and tactical suppliers.
  • Price variance by item across all five suppliers to find consolidation targets.
  • Interactive Plotly dashboard covering category, monthly trend, supplier and ABC views.

How I work

  • I audit the data before trusting it. Truncated text, negative sales, repeated postings and regex false positives were all found by checking, not assuming.
  • I validate against a baseline. Models are compared to a naive benchmark, using grouped or time-aware splits where leakage is a risk.
  • I translate analysis into action. Each project ends with specific recommendations a business owner can act on.
  • I state limitations plainly. Sample size, assumptions and data gaps are part of the result.
